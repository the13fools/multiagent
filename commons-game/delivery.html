$275,000 · 18 months · five decision points

Build a laboratory for guiding flocks toward mutual flourishing.

The grant funds a rigorous programme for learning how fine-tuning and post-training shape long-horizon behaviour in multi-agent games, then turns that knowledge into a reusable evaluation and harness.

Milestone spine

Every quarter can change what happens next.

Month 3

Calibrate or retire the promotion rule.

30 live f = 0 campaigns of 60 matched pairs, across four strata. A pooled upper bound above 10% stops candidate use.

Month 6

Compare post-training channels and validate the classifier.

Prompts, LoRA and PDD face held-out action checks, an impossible regime, a style control and scripted majorities.

Month 9

Estimate f* or declare it undefined.

The powered N = 8 sweep gives an isotonic estimate with bootstrap intervals, a monotonicity test and a seed-versus-mimic contrast.

Month 12

Test transfer across games, tools, providers and scale.

The informative range repeats in Shared Resource, the tool-and-memory environment, at N = 20 and 50, across providers and against the frontier subset. All experiments complete.

Month 18

Release the laboratory and its conclusion.

Code, schemas, permitted traces, replication guide and synthesis cover every planned arm—nulls and voided strata included.

Team and readiness

The programme starts from working infrastructure.

Lead investigator. 1.0 FTE months 1–12, then 0.4 FTE. Built the current testbed, server, decision path, training pipeline, and visualizations independently with about $200 in compute.

Reproducibility engineer. 480 contracted hours, months 4–12, for serving, campaign orchestration, the tool-and-memory environment, validation, and release.

External review. The estimand, intervals, multiplicity, and missingness plan are reviewed before the first scored campaign.

Host. Eligible nonprofit sponsorship is pending; no current host commitment is implied.

Stage Zero The grant pays for calibration, power, transfer, and reproducibility—not a first prototype.

Award boundary

The instrument exists either way. The evidence does not.

Without this award, powered calibration, cross-provider transfer, and N = 20/50 do not happen on this schedule.

A null still yields a complete public evaluation; no further funding is sought for that release. Replicated transfer may motivate a separate institutional programme; forums and voting are not deliverables of this grant.

This is the most dual-use idea here. A mechanism that helps agents reach legitimate agreement can also manufacture apparent consensus. Measurement, provenance and adversarial evaluation must come first. No funds are requested for it.
