What is already built

Two chapters of theory, and then the part where somebody had to actually write it. This is Stage Zero: the code, the runs, and what they found.

Everything up to here has been an argument about a game. This page is the answer to the reasonable next question — is any of it real? Three repositories, all public, all MIT or CC0, roughly eleven thousand lines and sixty-five tests. They were written by one person over several months, on compute paid for out of pocket, and they have already produced results, two of which are inconvenient for the person who produced them.

Five results, and what each is worth

Stage Zero was not a demo. It was five measurements, of which the two most useful are embarrassing.

Why the awkward ones matter most. A gate that rolls back a clone of its own baseline is not a bug in someone else's system; it is the tool this proposal is built around, failing an obvious test that nobody had thought to run. Finding it took an afternoon. That is the argument for the whole approach in one line: in an environment with an answer key, an instrument can be caught being wrong, and in an environment without one it simply cannot.

The commons configuration is the same story at smaller scale. Ceiling-to-upkeep sat at 0.39 for weeks, meaning collapse was guaranteed for every strategy, so every cell in that environment was measuring nothing. That is invisible without ground truth and obvious with it.

Who built it, and why the timing

The applicant

Who One independent researcher, recently finished a PhD. No lab, no institutional backing, no current or pending funding from anywhere.
What was spent Several months of full-time work and compute out of pocket. That constraint is visible in the design and not by accident: a judge panel was never in budget, so the evaluation is arithmetic; re-rolling a teacher was never in budget, so the edit is cached once and every arm consumes the same immutable copy, which turned a cost measure into a fairness guarantee.
Why now Agent populations are being deployed across shared infrastructure faster than the instruments to measure them exist. Testbeds are the bottleneck the rest of this agenda runs through, and building them after deployment is the wrong order.
What the funding changes Not whether the work happens — the instrument exists and stays public either way. It changes whether the campaign that would make its results statistically meaningful can run. A powered dose-response needs about 69 paired cells per contrast across two model families, which is not reachable on self-funded compute.

The work to date is the evidence that the next stage would be spent well. It is also the reason to think it needs doing now rather than later.

Where this leaves the proposal

The site is arranged in the order the argument is made.

Chapters 1–2
theory and setup		 The game and steering. A commons with a closed-form solution, and the question of how few agents you must control to hold a population you do not own.
This chapter
Stage Zero, done		 The instrument, built and public, and five results — including the two that say the instrument was broken in ways only an answer key could reveal.
Chapters after
the proposal		 How a campaign is run and what would make it interpretable; what has and has not run; and what the funding buys — a population that is a variable rather than a limitation, and the decentralised-mechanism question underneath it.

Two things situate the proposal beyond this site. The call's own starting points — Distributional AGI Safety, ARIA's Scaling Trust, and the Cooperative AI Foundation's multi-agent risks report — are answered one at a time on where this sits, in their vocabulary rather than ours: this is an impermeable sandbox economy, deliberately tiny, whose object of study is the oversight mechanism rather than the economy.

And the direction is a ladder of long-rollout text games, each rung trading away answer key for realism: solvable games now, the same games with a communication channel next, then Diplomacy — where full-press against gunboat is already the standard with-and-without-side-channels manipulation — and eventually populations at the scale of a twenty-year-old MMO, where the coordination happens on channels the engine cannot see. The reason to be strict about the bottom rung is that a result at the top only means something if the instrument reading it was calibrated somewhere the answer was known.
