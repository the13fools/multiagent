Do the others lock on?

A different question from whether the commons survives.

The Shared Resource game asks whether a population finds the sustainable rate. This asks something narrower: you have pinned some agents to the correct phase. Do the rest fall into step?

Those come apart. A flock can sit at the right average rate and still die, because the phases are wrong. A follower rule can be individually sensible and collectively unentrainable.

This is the page the whole site is for. Steering a flock you do not own means holding a population on a pattern using only the seats you control, and the number that matters is how few of those you need. Here it is computable, so a measurement of a real model has something to disagree with.

One run

You control some of the agents and hold them to the correct alternating phase. Everyone else runs a fixed rule. The question is whether the ones you do not control fall into step.

Read the rows. Some rules survive with nobody controlled. Imitation needs six of eight. Pure defection never entrains at all — there is no number of controlled seats short of every seat that helps.

Why imitation is the worst case

The pattern this game needs is anti-correlated: half restoring, half taking, every turn. Copying the majority does the opposite. It pulls the flock into lockstep, which is precisely the state that starves it.

That matters beyond this game, because imitation is the social heuristic language-model agents most reliably exhibit. Controllability is a property of the agents you do not control. How many seats you hold barely matters by comparison.
