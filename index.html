A game everyone can win, that nobody wins

Eight agents share a pool. There is a way for all of them to live forever. It is one sentence long. A flock of Qwen2.5-7B never finds it and is dead by turn 14.

The rules. Every turn each player pays 1 token to survive, then takes one action: pay 1 token to add 3 to the shared pool, or take 3 out of it. Run out of tokens and you are removed. That is the whole game.

The solution. Alternate. Restore, take, restore, take. Your balance holds level, the pool holds level, and nobody ever dies. This is arithmetic, not an equilibrium anyone hopes will emerge.

The finding. A flock of language models takes every turn and starves. The gap between a one-sentence solution and a population that walks past it is what flockbench measures.

Four things one small game gives you

A checkable answer. What an agent can afford to restore, and what the pool needs restored, both follow from the rules. Where they meet, everyone can live forever. Where they do not, nobody can.

A difficulty dial with an answer key. One parameter sets how many free-riders a flock carries, in closed form. Measured thresholds have something to be measured against.

Horizon as a variable. An agent slightly off the correct rate does not fail — it drifts, and dies later. Small biases are invisible at ten turns and fatal at two hundred.

Steering as entrainment. The sustainable pattern is a balanced two-colouring nobody assigns. The critical fraction depends more on how the others update than on how many seats you hold.
