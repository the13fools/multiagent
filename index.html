The problem with studying this in a rich environment is that you cannot tell a population that failed from one that faced an impossible problem. So these games are small enough that the best achievable outcome is computable exactly, and the interesting quantity becomes the distance between what a population did and what the arithmetic already permitted.

Every number on this site is computed from the rules of the game. Nothing is scored by a model. The engines are ports of flockbench, the research testbed where these games are played by live language models, and the test suite pins the two implementations to each other — so an illustration here cannot quietly drift away from the experiment it illustrates.

Four things that fall out of one small game

The answer is checkable. The share of turns an agent can afford to spend restoring, and the share the pool needs restored, are both computable from the rules. Where they coincide, a strategy exists under which everyone lives forever. Where they do not, none does. A population's shortfall becomes a number rather than an opinion.

Difficulty has a dial with an answer key. One parameter changes exactly how many free-riders a flock can carry, and that count is closed-form — so a measured threshold has ground truth to be compared against, which is rare.

Long rollouts are dynamics. A long episode is a numerical integration and the agents are the integrator. An agent running slightly off the correct rate does not fail immediately; it drifts and dies later. Small per-step biases are invisible at ten turns and fatal at two hundred, which is a concrete argument against extrapolating from short runs.

Steering is entrainment. The sustainable pattern is a balanced two-colouring of the agent-by-turn grid, and nobody assigns it. That makes this a coupled-oscillator problem with a forced subpopulation — and the critical fraction depends far more on how the followers update than on how many seats you hold.

Where the work actually stands

Research sites usually describe the version of a project that will exist once everything works. Below is the one that exists. The status page carries the full accounting, including five known problems we have not fixed.

  
measured Shared Resource environment, closed-form solution and carrying capacity, asserted in two independent implementations
measured Stage Zero pilot — 30 matched cells, zero parse failures, and the gate audit that came out of it
simulated Conformist populations need 75% of seats to steer. True of scripted rules; no language model has been measured against it
resampled The gate's false-rejection rate, and the campaign size at which non-inferiority passes
not run Live A/A calibration, the powered dose-response campaign, and anything above N = 8
